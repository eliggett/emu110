Roland SN-R8 CARD DECODE (tools/r8_extract.py)     <- neither hardware nor a MAME render

These are not recordings and not emulator output.  They are the sample data on a Roland
SN-R8 PCM card, decoded straight out of the ROM image by tools/r8_extract.py: descramble,
expand the 8-bit float, integrate the deltas, write at the engine's 32 kHz.  No envelope,
no pitch shift, no output filter, peak-normalised per file.

They exist to answer one question by ear -- does the U-110's decoder read R-8 data
correctly? -- and the answer is yes.  See analysis/R8-CONVERSION.md.

    sn-r8-10-dance/     SN-R8-10 "Dance", six of its twenty-six tones

Each R-8 tone carries TWO sample blocks and the machine sounds both, so each tone gives
three files:

    NN_NAME_A.wav       block A alone -- the attack and body
    NN_NAME_B.wav       block B alone -- the low end, where the tone has one
    NN_NAME.wav         A + B, which is roughly what the R-8 makes

04_909_S is the clearest illustration: A is the snare's noise at 5.3 kHz, B is the shell
tone at 132 Hz, and only the sum is a snare drum.  12_909_T splits the same way, 1.2 kHz
against 68 Hz.  13_909_CHH is noise in both blocks, as a hi-hat should be.  17_RVB_CLP has
no B block at all.

Source dumps live outside the repo, in ~/Downloads/roland_sn-u-stuff_for-elliott/ .

--- via-card/ -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The same R-8 samples played BY THE MACHINE: converted into a U-110 card with
tools/r8_to_u110.py, mounted in slot 1, rendered through the emulator's own audio path.
Unlike the files above -- Python decoding ROM bytes directly -- these went through the real
firmware, the real tone generator and the real output stage.

    sn-r8-10_kit.wav     SN-R8-10 "Dance", notes 36..57 (SET1) and 58..61 (SET2)
    sn-r8-09_kit.wav     SN-R8-09 "Power Drums USA"
    sn-r8-11_kit.wav     SN-R8-11 "Metallic Percussion" -- keys 43 and 48 are the same
                         crash as key 42, pitched +1 and +7 semitones
    sn-r8-07_kit.wav     SN-R8-07 "Mallet"
    custom_kit.wav       a kit assembled from THREE cards (tools/r8_kit_example.txt),
                         including one ATAK_T1 tom on four keys at -5/0/+4/+8 semitones,
                         stored as a single sample

One note per second, chromatically, velocity 110, on a single-part patch (preset 1
"Ac.Piano", parts 2-6 disabled) so nothing but the card is sounding.

All 22 keys of a kit speak, in order, each at its own natural pitch, and the keys outside
the map are silent -- which is what a U-110 drum tone should do.  Both R-8 sample blocks
are summed onto each key, so a kick keeps its boom and a snare its shell tone.

This needed the drum key map: a U-110 drum tone does not compare the MIDI note against its
split points at all, it looks the note up in a 128-byte table first.  See
analysis/R8-DRUM-TONES.md.

All of these are rendered with the 6 ms tail fade described in R8-CONVERSION.md section
5.6.  Without it a cymbal fades out and then clicks: the firmware shuts a finished one-shot
with a 0.85 ms envelope slam and the de-emphasis turns the step into a thump.  R-8 samples
do not reach silence by their last byte, so there is always something for it to cut.
